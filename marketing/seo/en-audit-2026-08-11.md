# Gridfin EN SEO/SRO Audit

## Verdict

The English content was substantially complete but undiscoverable: all three
priority URLs returned 404 on production and were absent from the root sitemap
and llms.txt. The source also leaked a Russia-specific FAQ and an RF-hosted
form endpoint into EN output. The repo-side blockers are now fixed without
changing the landing design.

## Evidence and fixes

| Area | Before | Fix | Retest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reachability | EN landing and both supporting pages: 404 | Static EN bundle integrated into the `.dev` Next app with market-scoped rewrites | Local build/runtime pending full gate |
| Canonicals | Generated directory-style trailing slashes conflicted with Next's slashless URLs | EN canonicals, hreflang and sitemap URLs are slashless; RU stays unchanged | Landing tests green |
| Discovery | Gridfin absent from root sitemap and llms.txt | Three priority URLs added to both; EN-only Gridfin sitemap contains exactly three `<loc>` entries | Unit tests green |
| Market isolation | EN FAQ mentioned Russia; EN form posted to Yandex Cloud | FAQ replaced with tool requirements; EN form uses same-origin Resend API; RU backend unchanged | Source/bundle regression assertions green |
| Supporting pages | Description and canonical only | OG/Twitter metadata plus Article or TechArticle and BreadcrumbList JSON-LD | Landing tests green |
| Form boundary | Static cross-origin endpoint, no honeypot on EN | zod strict schema, honeypot, IP rate limit and existing Resend delivery | Route tests green |
| Legal market split | EN Terms described the RU Yandex/RF data flow | Separate International Beta Terms under Spanish law; RU Terms remain RU-only; privacy points to the existing `.dev` notice | Source and copied-bundle regression tests green |
| Competitive copy | Claimed competitors only had unenforced rule files | Reframed around fit and Gridfin's enforceable end-to-end process | Primary competitor pages checked 2026-08-11 |

## Semantic assessment

- Central search intent is explicit in title, H1, hero support and FAQ.
- Application Skeleton is consistently defined as the central entity and has
  its own definition page.
- The landing covers category, problem, mechanism, fit, evidence, objections
  and conversion in a coherent H1→H2 flow.
- Direct-answer FAQ passages are suitable for extraction; JSON-LD mirrors the
  visible answers.
- The current three-page network is intentionally compact. Its next real gap
  is specification-first development, not another broad “AI coding” article.

## Remaining risks

1. Production remains 404 until the Ludvik4 changes are explicitly published.
2. Search engines and LLM search surfaces cannot discover or cite an
   unpublished source; no ranking or citation outcome is promised.
3. The early-access offer has placeholder post-beta prices. A pricing page or
   comparison page should not launch until commercial terms are stable.
4. Competitors now publish heavily against “Claude Code boilerplate” and
   related queries. On-site copy alone will not create third-party consensus.
5. The RU bundle's EN hreflang becomes fully honest only after the `.dev`
   publication is live.
